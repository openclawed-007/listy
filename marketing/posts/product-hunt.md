## Maker's first comment

```
Hi PH 👋

I made CartLink because every shared shopping list app my household tried died at the same step: getting everyone else to download the thing and make an account. The list ended up on one phone and we went back to texting "did you say oat or almond?".

So CartLink does the sharing differently. You turn on sharing and get a link and a QR code. Stick the QR on the fridge. Anyone who scans it is looking at the live list on their own phone and can tick things off or add to it — with no account at all, if you allow it. You stay in charge: guests can never delete items, and you can switch sharing off whenever you like.

The bits I'm quietly proud of:
• Real-time sync that actually keeps up in the shop
• Optional quantities and aisles — the list groups itself when you use them, stays flat when you don't
• Works offline in the basement supermarket, syncs when you surface
• Undo delete, because thumbs slip in the queue
• A fully native Android app (Kotlin / Jetpack Compose) that shares the same list
• Dark mode that remembers, no white flash

What it doesn't do: no ads, no selling your data, no premium tier. It's a small independent project and the code is open on GitHub.

Honest gaps: sign-in for list owners is Google-only right now, and the Android app is an APK from GitHub rather than the Play Store. Both are on the list.

I'd love to know what would make you actually stick the QR on your fridge. I'll be here all day.
```

## Replies you'll need

**"iOS app?"**
> Not yet. The website installs to the iPhone home screen and works offline, which covers most of it. A native iOS app depends on whether enough people want it — you're a data point, thank you.

**"How is this different from [Bring / AnyList / Google Keep / Listonic]?"**
> Most of them are more feature-rich than CartLink and that's fine. The one thing I'd claim: the person who scans the fridge code needs zero setup. No download, no account. That's the whole bet.

**"Is it really free? What's the catch?"**
> Really free. The catch is that it's a small project run by a person, not a company — so no SLA, no support team, just an email address that I read.
